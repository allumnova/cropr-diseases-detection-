1. Plain-Language System Summary (How to Explain to the Professor)

Traditional crop monitoring fails because manual inspection is slow, while image-only AI models miss environmental triggers (humidity, dew, soil dryness) and sensor-only IoT nodes cannot confirm which exact pathogen is present. Our system solves both together in a 4-stage electrical + AI pipeline:

30-Second Viva Pitch
“Sir, we built an implementation-ready AIoT + Electrical Support prototype. Stage 1 continuously monitors temperature, humidity, soil moisture, light, and NPK to compute a Disease Risk Score—keeping the camera and pump asleep to save battery. When risk crosses the threshold or an ultrasonic sensor detects a plant, Stage 2 triggers the ESP32-CAM to capture a leaf image and classify the disease using an open-source MobileNetV2 + Grad-CAM model. Stage 3 passes that decision through 5 electrical safety interlocks before driving a 12V pump via an IRLZ44N MOSFET. Stage 4 uses an INA219 sensor to measure bus voltage, current, power, and actuator response time.”

4. Four Functional Variations & Field Control Scope

The hardware-software architecture is modular—meaning the same rover and sensor node handles four distinct agricultural control problems:

1. Pest & Fungal Disease Control

Triggers ESP32-CAM when humidity > 80% and LWI > 65%. Confirms visible lesions via CNN + Grad-CAM and actuates targeted fungicide mist at 75–85% MOSFET PWM duty.

2. Dryness / Irrigation Control

When Capacitive Soil Moisture drops below 30% during high solar Lux/temperature, the system triggers a clean-water irrigation pulse without dispensing chemical pesticide.

3. Fertilizer (NPK) Control

RS485 Soil NPK sensor detects Nitrogen/Potassium deficits and issues crop-specific foliar advisories—and blocks excess Nitrogen in Rice Blast cases to stop fungal spread.

4. Humidity & Canopy Micro-Climate Control

Computes Dew-Point Spread and Leaf Wetness Index (LWI) from DHT22 + BH1750 to warn farmers before spore germination occurs, advising overhead irrigation pause.

5. Electrical Engineering Core & 5 Mandatory Safety Interlocks

Power Distribution & Protection
  • Source: 12V Li-ion/LiFePO4 Battery Pack with BMS + Main Switch + 5A Fuse.
  • Regulation: LM2596 DC-DC Buck Converter steps 12V down to 5.0V (ESP32, Servos, HC-SR04) and 3.3V logic.
  • Actuator Drive: IRLZ44N Logic-Level MOSFET switches the 12V pump with a 1N4007 flyback diode to suppress inductive spikes.
  • EE Measurement: INA219 I2C shunt logs Voltage (V), Current (A), Power (W), Energy (Wh), and latency (ms).
5 Safety Interlocks Before Pump Activation
  1. AI Confidence Gate: Prediction confidence must be ≥ 75%.
  2. Battery Voltage Gate: Bus voltage ≥ 10.8V (prevents BMS deep-discharge cutoff).
  3. Tank Level Gate: Reservoir ≥ 10% (prevents dry-running the 12V pump).
  4. Cooldown Gate: ≥ 30s between sprays (prevents chemical over-application).
  5. Mode / E-Stop Gate: System must be in AUTO mode (no manual override / E-Stop).
